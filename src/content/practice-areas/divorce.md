### Requirements of Divorce in Texas

Most states require a ‘cooling-off’ period. In Texas, this period is 60 days. This means that once a formal petition for divorce is filed, the case cannot be completely finalized until the 61st day it has been on file. This waiting period is intended to provide time for reflection and negotiation, which sometimes aids in reconciling differences or settling disputes out of court.

Additionally, [Texas has certain residency requirements](/family-law/divorce/residency-requirement/) that must be met before someone can file for divorce. At least one party must be a resident of the state of Texas for at least 6 months and a resident of that county for 90 days before a petition for divorce can be filed. Understanding these prerequisites helps prepare an appropriate legal strategy.

### Contested vs. Uncontested Divorce in Pearland

There are two main [types of divorces](/family-law/divorce/right-type-divorce/) in Pearland, Texas: contested or uncontested.

**A contested divorce means the parties cannot agree on important matters, such as:**

- [Splitting retirement accounts](/family-law/qdros/)
- Selling the marital house
- Determining child custody and support
- Dividing marital assets and debts

An uncontested divorce occurs when the parties can agree on all aspects of the divorce and they simply need to memorialize this agreement in a final court order. While they can do this without the assistance of a Pearland divorce lawyer, it is recommended that at least one party retains a lawyer to ensure the paperwork is drafted appropriately and that all documents are drafted to ensure the conveyance of property (like deeds).

Uncontested divorces tend to be less expensive and time-consuming than contested cases. They offer a pathway to settle disputes amicably and often require fewer legal proceedings, which can be beneficial in maintaining respectful family dynamics post-divorce. Our firm can assist in ensuring that even uncontested divorces proceed smoothly, safeguarding against potential complications in documentation or execution of agreements.

### How Is Property Divided in a Texas Divorce?

In Texas, property division during a divorce follows the principle of community property. This means that any assets acquired during the marriage are generally considered joint property and are subject to division. However, this does not always result in an exact 50/50 split. Instead, the court aims to divide property in a manner that is "just and right," taking into account various factors that may affect fairness.

Community property includes income earned by either spouse during the marriage, homes purchased, retirement accounts, and other assets accumulated while married. On the other hand, separate property—which is not subject to division—includes assets owned before the marriage, gifts, inheritances, and certain personal injury settlements. It’s important to note that separate property must be clearly proven, or it may be presumed as community property by the court.

When dividing community property, the court may consider factors such as the length of the marriage, each spouse's financial situation, fault in the breakup of the marriage (such as adultery or cruelty), and the future needs of each party, especially if children are involved. ## Commonly Asked Questions

### What Are the Residency Requirements for Filing for Divorce in Texas?

At least one party must be a resident of the state of Texas for at least 6 months and a resident of that county for 90 days before a petition for divorce can be filed.

### Can You Get a Divorce in Texas Without Going to Court?

Yes, getting a divorce in Texas without going to court is possible. The divorce must be amicable, and attorneys should represent both parties to do so. Such arrangements prevent time-consuming court proceedings and foster a cooperative environment, ensuring fair negotiation and agreement documentation.

### Can a Spouse Refuse a Divorce in Texas?

Yes. Texas is a no-fault divorce state, meaning a divorce can be granted even if one spouse doesn't agree. This law means that if a spouse refuses to sign divorce papers, it does not prevent a divorce.

### What Is a Wife Entitled to In a Divorce in Texas?

**In general, a wife in a Texas divorce case is entitled to:**

- An equal division of the community property
- Spousal support
- A portion of her husband's retirement benefits
- A proper and appropriate custody arrangement for any child from the marriage
- Equal assets and properties
- Separate property

### How Long Does a Divorce Process Take in Texas?

The duration of a divorce process in Texas varies widely depending on the complexity of the issues involved and the level of agreement between the parties. An uncontested divorce could be finalized as soon as the 61-day waiting period concludes. However, contentious divorces involving disputes over property division, custody, or significant assets can take several months to over a year. Factors such as court availability, the necessity for evaluations (like home studies), or asset appraisals also affect the timeline.
