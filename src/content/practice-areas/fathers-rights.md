## What Are My Rights as an Unmarried Father in Texas?

Unmarried fathers in Texas must establish [paternity](/family-law/paternity/) to gain legal rights concerning their children. Once paternity is established, fathers can seek to have their names added to the child's birth certificate and assert their rights to custody and visitation. Establishing paternity also obligates fathers to provide child support if needed. The legal team at The Dieye Firm can assist unmarried fathers in navigating the paternity establishment process and advocating for involvement in their child's life.

Furthermore, understanding the full scope of rights that come with paternity beyond just visitation and custody is vital. Fathers can engage in parental decision-making regarding education, healthcare, and religious upbringing once paternity is established. It is essential for unmarried fathers to consult with a legal professional to fully understand these rights and further explore how best to protect their parental interests within the framework of Texas law.

## How Does Texas Law Protect Fathers' Rights During Divorce?

Under Texas law, fathers have the same rights as mothers in [divorce proceedings](/family-law/divorce/divorce-modification/), ensuring that they are given a fair opportunity to be involved in their children's lives. This includes access to housing, education, and medical decisions. Texas courts favor custody arrangements that allow for equal parental involvement whenever possible. During divorce proceedings, fathers are encouraged to engage with legal professionals to protect their rights and interests comprehensively. At The Dieye Firm, we advocate for fair treatment and equitable solutions for fathers navigating divorce.

**Important protections and considerations for fathers during divorce include:**

- **Equal standing in custody** determinations, with courts focusing on the child’s best interests rather than automatically favoring one parent.
- **Opportunities in temporary orders** hearings to establish parenting time, support, and property arrangements while the case is pending.
- **Financial rights and obligations** related to child support and property division that must be evaluated in light of Texas community property principles.

Additionally, fathers should be aware of their right to participate in temporary orders hearings that frequently occur during divorce proceedings. These hearings address immediate needs concerning [child custody](/family-law/child-custody/), support, and property, impacting the family’s interim financial distribution until a divorce is finalized. Being proactively involved in these discussions can set a precedent for final custody and support determinations. Our attorneys guide fathers in preparing and presenting their cases effectively within these preliminary negotiations.
