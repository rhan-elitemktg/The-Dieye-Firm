Prenuptial agreements are a relatively new presence in [Texas family law](/family-law/). Although they have been around for a while, they have been used more frequently in the past decade or so. Couples are delaying marriage, often to the point where they have established lucrative careers or acquired a decent amount of property on their own. Perhaps one spouse is earning significantly more than the other at the time of the marriage, or one spouse has incurred a lot of debt. In those cases, it is often a practical exercise to create a prenuptial agreement that determines how their property will be characterized and divided in the event of a [divorce](/family-law/divorce/).

## What is a Prenuptial Agreement?

**A prenuptial agreement is a legal contract between two parties that is executed prior to the marriage**. Prenups often clarify whether or not property acquired during the marriage will be considered community or separate property in the event of divorce. A thorough prenuptial agreement will also specify how property bought by the parties before the marriage will be owned. In most cases, it covers the potential division of retirement, real properties, and other assets in the marriage if the parties divorce.

These agreements can also contain provisions that require the payment of attorney’s fees, especially if one party challenges the agreement and loses. Parties can be quite creative when it comes to prenuptial agreements, although there are limitations to what such an agreement can accomplish.

Additionally, a well-crafted prenup might include provisions for dealing with any business interests owned by either party prior to marriage, ensuring that these businesses are protected as separate property unless explicitly mentioned otherwise. This aspect highlights the importance of individualized premarital planning, allowing couples to focus on safeguarding what truly matters to them.

### Defining Community vs. Separate Property in Texas

Income is automatically considered to be community property under default statutes, but a prenuptial agreement can change this and characterize all income from separate property or separate labor as not part of the community estate. It also can contemplate the ability of either party to request [spousal support](/family-law/spousal-support/). Furthermore, Texas law allows prenuptial agreements to delineate the terms for managing income derived from investments or retirement accounts accumulated during the marriage. By detailing these specifics in a prenup, couples can avoid potential disputes about ownership and claims to these assets post-divorce, thereby ensuring a clear understanding of financial expectations and responsibilities.

### Can Prenups Establish Child Custody & Support?

Prenuptial agreements can discuss parenting roles; however, the provisions determining custody or [child support](/family-law/child-support/) will not be binding in a court of law. The court will always consider the best interest of the child over and above a private contractual agreement. Parents can, however, outline their shared vision for parenting philosophies, including provisions for educational choices and religious upbringing, which may provide a framework for cooperative parenting. By setting these intentions early, parents demonstrate a commitment to co-parenting even in the event of separation, possibly minimizing future conflicts and illustrating a unified approach to raising their children.

### What Are the Limits of a Prenuptial Agreement?

One major thing parties should know is that a prenuptial agreement cannot waive a future spouse’s benefit under an ERISA-protected plan, like a 401k. The spouses must reaffirm any waiver of these retirement benefits after the date of marriage in order for these provisions to be enforced. It also cannot be used to defraud creditors, like transferring all the property you own into your spouse’s separate property in order to avoid foreclosure.

Of course, a prenup also cannot require anything unconscionable or illegal. Additionally, a prenuptial agreement cannot make determinations about personal rights unrelated to financial matters, such as dictating family routines or personal behaviors. These limitations ensure that the agreements remain focused on economic and property issues and reinforce the principle that certain aspects of a marriage cannot be predetermined by contract.

### What Are the Requirements of a Prenuptial Agreement?

In order for a prenuptial agreement to be enforced in the state of Texas:

- It must be in writing and signed by both parties.
- There must have been a full disclosure by both spouses of assets and liabilities prior to either of them executing the agreement.
- They must also waive the right to any further disclosure by the other party.

There are only two methods by which a spouse can challenge the enforceability of the contract as a whole:

- The agreement was signed involuntarily.
- The agreement was unconscionable at the time the agreement was signed, and the spouse challenging the agreement did not get a fair and reasonable disclosure of the property of the other spouse, did not voluntarily waive the right to disclose the assets, and did not have or could not have had adequate knowledge of the assets and liabilities of their future spouse.
- They were either forced to sign the agreement, or their future spouse tried to hide assets or debts from them.

Beyond the basic requirements, there are strategic factors to consider to ensure the agreement’s strength against challenges. Engaging in individual legal counsel for the drafting and signing of the prenup can underline its fairness and voluntary nature. It’s advisable for both parties to fully understand the terms to prevent future contestations. In addition, having the agreement reviewed periodically to reflect changes in circumstances or laws can sustain its relevance over time, solidifying its standing in potential enforcement scenarios.
