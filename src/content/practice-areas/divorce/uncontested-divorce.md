## What Makes a Texas Divorce Uncontested

Texas classifies a divorce as uncontested when both spouses agree on every issue the court must resolve: property and debt division, [spousal maintenance](/family-law/spousal-support/) if applicable, and, when children are involved, parenting time, decision-making authority, and child support calculated in accordance with Texas guidelines. If even one issue remains unresolved, the case may no longer qualify for the uncontested path.

Texas presumes all property acquired during the marriage is [community property](/family-law/property-division/). In an uncontested divorce, spouses may allocate that property as they choose, and the court approves the marital settlement agreement if it finds it fair and just. Avoiding a contested trial can reduce conflict and limit time spent in litigation, though actual outcomes depend on the complexity of each case.

## Children & Property in an Uncontested Divorce

When children are involved, the final decree must include a parenting plan that addresses [possession schedules](/family-law/child-custody/visitation-possession/), decision-making authority, and child support calculated under Texas guidelines. Courts require any agreed parenting arrangement to serve the best interest of the child, even when both parents are in full agreement. Parents in an uncontested case may propose a customized schedule rather than the Standard Possession Order, provided it meets that standard.

On the property side, the decree should address the marital home, vehicles, bank accounts, retirement accounts, and debts with language precise enough to prevent future disputes. Retirement accounts and 401(k) contributions made during the marriage are typically community property and may require a separate [qualified domestic relations order](/family-law/qdros/) to be divided correctly under federal law. ## Waiver of Service in a Cooperative Divorce

When both spouses are working together, the respondent can sign a waiver of service, eliminating the need for formal service of process through a process server or constable. This can reduce both time and cost. Signing a waiver doesn’t waive the respondent’s right to receive a copy of the final decree or to review the agreed terms before they’re submitted to the court.

## Texas Community Property Basics

Texas is a community property state, meaning all property acquired during the marriage is presumed to belong equally to both spouses and is subject to division. Separate property (&mdash; assets owned before the marriage or received as a gift or inheritance during it (&mdash; generally isn’t subject to division, but proving that status requires clear documentation. In an uncontested divorce, spouses can divide community property however they agree, as long as the court finds the arrangement fair and just. Retirement accounts, pension plans, and 401(k) contributions made during the marriage are typically community property and must be addressed in the decree or through a [qualified domestic relations order](/family-law/qdros/).

## Agreeing on Child Custody & Support in Pearland

Texas courts require that any agreed parenting plan serve the best interest of the child, even when the divorce is uncontested. Parents may agree to a customized schedule that differs from the Standard Possession Order, provided the court finds it serves the child’s best interest.

The Standard Possession Order gives non-primary parents the following baseline schedule:

- Possession on the first, third, and fifth weekends of each month
- A Thursday midweek period during the school year
- Alternating holidays
- 30 days of extended summer possession

Texas [child support](/family-law/child-support/) follows a statutory formula based on the paying parent’s net monthly resources and the number of children. The guideline formula doesn’t factor in the other parent’s income or the possession schedule. Child support is considered the right of the child and cannot be waived by the receiving parent.
