## Understanding the Divorce Process in Texas:

## 1. Familiarize Yourself with the Residency Requirements:

- To file for divorce in Texas, either you or your spouse must have been a resident of the state for at least six months, and a resident of the county where you plan to file for at least 90 days.

## 2. Consider the Type of Divorce:

- In Texas, you can opt for either a contested or uncontested divorce. An uncontested divorce is typically faster and less expensive, as both parties agree on all aspects of the divorce. On the other hand, a contested divorce may take longer if disagreements arise regarding child custody, property division, or alimony.

## 3. Gather All Necessary Documents:

- Before filing for divorce, ensure you have all the necessary documents, such as marriage certificates, financial records, and any prenuptial or postnuptial agreements. Organizing these documents beforehand can save you time and streamline the divorce process.

## 4. Seek Mediation or Collaborative Divorce:

- Mediation and collaborative divorce are alternative dispute resolution methods that can help expedite the divorce process. These approaches focus on negotiation and cooperation, allowing you and your spouse to reach mutually beneficial agreements outside of court.

## 5. Understand the Waiting Period:

- In Texas, there is a mandatory waiting period of 60 days from the date the divorce petition is filed before a divorce can be finalized. However, keep in mind that complex divorces or disputes may extend this timeframe.
