If you and your ex-spouse have agreed to shared custody of your child, you may be wondering if you still have to pay child support. The short answer is yes, but the amount you pay may be different depending on several factors. In this blog post, we will explore the ins and outs of child support payments in shared custody arrangements and provide some useful tips to help you navigate this complex issue.

## 1. How is child support calculated in shared custody arrangements?

The amount of child support you are required to pay in a shared custody arrangement will depend on several factors, including the income of both parents, the number of children involved, and the amount of time each parent spends with the child. In general, the parent with the higher income will be required to pay more child support, but the exact amount will be calculated based on a formula established by state law.

## 2. Can child support payments be modified in shared custody arrangements?

Yes, child support payments can be modified in shared custody arrangements if there is a significant change in circumstances, such as a job loss or a change in the amount of time each parent spends with the child. It is important to work with an experienced family law attorney to ensure that any modifications to child support payments are handled correctly and in compliance with state law.

## 3. What happens if one parent fails to pay child support in a shared custody arrangement?

If one parent fails to pay child support in a shared custody arrangement, the other parent can take legal action to enforce the child support order. This may involve wage garnishment, seizure of assets, or other legal remedies. It is important to work with an experienced family law attorney to ensure that your rights are protected in these situations.

In conclusion, if you have shared custody of your child, you are still required to pay child support. However, the amount you pay may be different depending on several factors. It is important to work with an experienced family law attorney to ensure that your child support payments are fair and in compliance with state law.
